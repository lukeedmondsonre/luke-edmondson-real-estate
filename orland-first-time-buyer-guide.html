First-Time Home Buyer Guide: What to Know Before Buying in Orland, CA

Orland offers one of the most accessible price points in Northern California. Here's what first-time buyers need to know about Glenn County's market, down payment programs, and what to expect from local properties.

For first-time buyers who've been crunching numbers against Chico or Redding prices, Orland tends to stand out. Glenn County's median home values run well below the statewide average, and Orland's community character, location near Interstate 5, and steady local housing stock make it a legitimate option for buyers who want a foothold in Northern California real estate without overextending themselves.

That said, buying in a smaller agricultural market like Orland comes with its own set of considerations. Property types vary widely, rural homes have specific due diligence requirements, and the market has shifted over the past year in ways that matter to first-time buyers. Here's an informed look at what you'll find.

Where the Orland Market Stands in 2026

The most recent data from Redfin shows Orland home sales averaging around $370,000, with some listings coming in higher depending on size, acreage, and condition. Over a recent three-month period, Orland prices were up about 22% year over year, reflecting demand for affordable Northern California inventory as buyers from higher-cost areas seek alternatives. Glenn County as a whole has a median closer to $336,000, which gives a sense of what more rural or smaller parcels in the area trade for.

At Orland's price point, though, the math tends to work more favorably than in markets where prices have stayed at $500,000 or above. A $370,000 purchase with a standard conventional loan puts your principal and interest payment at a range that many buyers in this income bracket can work with, especially when paired with down payment assistance programs.

Down Payment Programs Worth Knowing

One of the most common barriers first-time buyers face is accumulating a down payment while also managing rent. California has several programs that can reduce or eliminate that barrier, and Orland buyers can access most of them.

CalHFA MyHome

California's primary down payment assistance program offers a deferred-payment loan for up to 3.5% of the purchase price. It can be layered with FHA financing and is designed specifically for first-time buyers at qualifying income levels.

FHA Loans

FHA financing requires as little as 3.5% down and has more flexible credit requirements than conventional loans. Many first-time buyers in rural California markets use FHA as their primary financing vehicle.

USDA Rural Loans

Parts of Glenn County qualify for USDA Rural Development loans, which offer 0% down financing for eligible buyers and properties. Income and geographic limits apply, but Orland's rural character means more properties here qualify than in Chico or Redding.

CalHFA Zero Interest

CalHFA also offers a zero-interest second mortgage to cover closing costs for first-time buyers who meet income requirements. Combined with MyHome, this program can significantly reduce how much cash you need at the closing table.

Pre-approval is the essential first step before any of this becomes concrete. Knowing which programs you qualify for, and what your actual purchasing power looks like with them, changes how you approach the search entirely. What Orland Is Like as a Community

Orland is a small agricultural city of about 8,200 people in Glenn County, situated along Interstate 5 roughly 100 miles north of Sacramento. It has deep roots in farming, with olive cultivation and honey production among its most recognized industries. The area is sometimes called the "Olive City," and the surrounding Glenn County is one of the country's more significant producers of honeybee colonies.

That agricultural identity shapes what the community feels like: unhurried, practical, and close-knit. There are local schools, a small downtown commercial area, and easy access to nearby Chico for larger shopping, healthcare, and entertainment. For buyers who want space and quiet without sacrificing highway access, Orland's location near I-5 is a genuine advantage.

The surrounding Glenn County also offers outdoor recreation at Black Butte Lake, a reservoir managed by the Army Corps of Engineers about 10 miles west of Orland. For buyers who want water access, fishing, and camping within a short drive of home, the area delivers that without the pricing premium of communities closer to Lake Oroville or Mount Shasta.

Property Types and What First-Time Buyers Should Know

Orland's housing stock is diverse. In town, you'll find standard single-family homes on city lots, many of them built from the 1950s through the 1990s. On the edges of the city and in the broader county, properties shift toward agricultural parcels, hobby farms, and rural homesites that can include outbuildings, acreage, and older infrastructure.

For first-time buyers, a few things are worth understanding before making an offer:

  • Septic systems: Many Orland properties, particularly those outside city limits, are served by private septic systems rather than municipal sewer. A septic inspection as part of your due diligence is important. Costs to repair or replace a failed system can be significant.
  • Water supply: In-town Orland properties are generally on city water. Rural properties vary. Some are on public utility connections, while wells are common on agricultural parcels. Understanding the water source and well condition matters for budgeting and loan eligibility.
  • Older homes: Many of Orland's in-town homes were built several decades ago. A thorough inspection is a highly encouraged step. Older electrical systems, plumbing, and roofing can show up as items to negotiate on or budget for after closing.
  • Agricultural zoning: If you're considering a property with acreage or agricultural designations, it's worth understanding what that zoning allows and restricts. Some buyers are drawn to the idea of a small property with land, and that can work well, but the due diligence process is a bit different from a standard in-town purchase.

Steps for First-Time Buyers in Orland

The process is the same regardless of where you buy, but the specifics matter. Here's the sequence that tends to work well for first-time buyers in this market:

  • Get pre-approved with a lender who knows Northern California rural markets and the programs available to first-time buyers.
  • Define what matters most: in-town versus rural, lot size, condition of the home, school district, commute distance.
  • Start searching with realistic expectations. Orland's inventory is smaller than Chico's, so patience matters. The right property may take a few weeks to find.
  • Make an offer with your inspection contingency in place.
  • Close and take possession. In California, escrow typically runs 30 to 45 days after acceptance.

Each step sounds simple but has details that matter. Having an agent who knows Glenn County's property landscape, lenders, and local inspection resources makes each step faster and less stressful. That's exactly the kind of support I provide to buyers I work with.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Orland a good place to buy a first home?
For buyers who value a small-town feel, lower price points than Chico or Redding, and I-5 access, Orland can be an excellent fit. The current market gives buyers time to be deliberate, and the availability of programs like USDA and CalHFA makes the entry numbers more workable than in higher-priced markets.
Can I use a USDA loan in Orland?
Parts of Glenn County qualify for USDA Rural Development loan programs, which offer 0% down financing for eligible buyers. Eligibility depends on the specific property location and your household income. A lender who works with USDA loans can confirm whether a specific property qualifies before you invest time in the process.
How long does it take to buy a home in Orland?
With homes averaging 44 to 55 days on market, you typically have more time to search and evaluate than in Chico or Redding. Once you're under contract, California escrow runs approximately 30 to 45 days. Getting pre-approved before you start searching compresses the overall timeline.
Do I need to worry about septic systems when buying in Orland?
It depends on the property. In-town Orland homes are generally on municipal sewer. Rural and agricultural properties are more likely to have private septic systems. For any property with a septic system, a septic inspection is a standard and important part of the due diligence process.
What's the difference between buying in Orland vs. Chico for a first-time buyer?
Chico's market is more active and carries a higher median price (around $467,000 in 2025-2026), which means larger down payments and higher monthly costs. Orland's lower price point, slower market pace, and USDA eligibility in some areas give first-time buyers advantages that don't exist in Chico. The tradeoff is a smaller inventory and a more rural setting.
What down payment do I need to buy in Orland?
Conventional loans typically require 3% to 20% down. FHA loans require 3.5%. USDA loans in qualifying areas require 0% down. CalHFA's MyHome program can cover the down payment for eligible buyers. The right program depends on your income, credit profile, and the specific property. A pre-approval conversation will sort out which option fits your situation.
