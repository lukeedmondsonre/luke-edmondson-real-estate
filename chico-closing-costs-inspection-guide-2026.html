Chico Closing Costs and Home Inspections: A 2026 Buyer's Guide

The price on the listing is only part of what it takes to buy a home. Here is how to plan for closing costs and inspections in Chico before you write an offer.

Most Chico buyers spend weeks watching list prices and monthly payments, then get surprised by a third number: the cash they need to bring to the closing table. With the average 30-year fixed rate at 7.28 percent as of October 1 and Chico homes selling near $479,000, knowing that number early can keep a good purchase from falling apart at the last minute.

As one of Northern California's top-producing Keller Williams agents and a Ramsey Trusted Partner, I can walk buyers through this before we ever look at a house. Let's break down what closing costs and inspections actually involve in Chico, and where you have room to negotiate.

What Closing Costs Include

Closing costs are the fees and prepaid items due when your loan funds and the home becomes yours. They are separate from your down payment. Published California estimates generally put them in the range of 2 to 5 percent of the purchase price, and your lender's Loan Estimate will show the real figures for your loan. On a $479,000 Chico home, that range works out to roughly $9,600 to $24,000.

The big categories are easy to understand once you see them side by side:

  • Lender fees: origination or underwriting charges, the credit report, and any discount points you choose to buy down your rate.
  • Title and escrow: title search, the lender's title insurance policy, escrow fees, and county recording fees.
  • Appraisal: typically several hundred dollars, ordered by your lender to confirm the home supports the loan amount.
  • Prepaids: your first year of homeowners insurance, a few months of property tax set aside in escrow, and interest from closing day to month end.

Prepaids are the piece that catches people off guard. They are not fees paid to someone for a service. They are your own future expenses collected up front, which is why the total can look large.

Plan for the Payment and the Cash

On a $479,000 purchase at 7.28 percent with 10 percent down, principal and interest is about $2,950 per month. With 5 percent down it is about $3,114. Your down payment, closing costs, and inspections all come from cash on hand, so we can sit down early and build the full picture together.

Home Inspections: What to Expect

The General Inspection

A general home inspection typically costs a few hundred dollars and takes a few hours. The inspector can look at the roof, foundation, plumbing, electrical system, heating and cooling, water heater, and visible signs of moisture or pests.

Specialty Inspections Worth Considering

Depending on the property, extra inspections can be worth the added cost. A sewer line camera inspection is smart on older Chico homes with mature trees. A roof inspection makes sense when the roof looks near the end of its life. A pest inspection is common in California transactions. Properties outside city limits on the edge of Chico may have a septic system, and that deserves its own evaluation. Chico summers are hot, so HVAC condition matters more than first-time buyers may expect.

Where You Can Negotiate

After the inspection, you generally have three options: ask the seller to make repairs, ask for a credit toward closing costs, or ask for a price reduction. Credits are often attractive in a higher-rate environment because they lower the cash you need on closing day. Lender rules limit how large a seller credit can be depending on your loan type and down payment, so we confirm those limits with your lender before we write the request.

You can also shop for the lender fees you control. Compare Loan Estimates from at least two or three lenders, and ask whether title and escrow companies have different fee schedules. For more on how today's rates change your options, read Buying a Home in Chico with High Interest Rates: 2026 Guide and Mortgage Rates in Northern California 2026: A Buyer's Guide.

A Simple Pre-Offer Checklist

  • Get pre-approved and ask your lender for a written estimate of closing costs.
  • Set aside cash for the down payment, closing costs, and inspections together.
  • Budget for the earnest money deposit, which is credited toward your purchase.
  • Schedule inspections quickly after your offer is accepted, since the contingency clock is running.
  • Get a homeowners insurance quote before you finalize your offer.

The Bottom Line

Closing costs and inspections do not have to be a surprise. When you know the range up front, you can choose a price point that works for your total cash, negotiate with confidence, and walk into closing day ready.
