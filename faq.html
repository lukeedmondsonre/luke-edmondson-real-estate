Buying a Home
The first step is getting pre-approved with a lender so you know your actual budget and can make competitive offers. From there, you define your priorities (neighborhood, size, commute), tour homes that fit, make an offer, complete inspections, and close. In Chico's market, pre-approval is especially important because well-priced homes move quickly. Luke can connect you with experienced local lenders and walk you through each step. Start with a conversation with Luke or read the first-time buyer guide for Chico.
From accepted offer to close of escrow typically takes 30 to 45 days in California. The time before that varies widely. In a competitive price range you may view homes for a few weeks and make multiple offers. In slower price points you might find and close in three to four weeks total. The biggest variable is loan type: conventional loans tend to close faster than FHA or VA loans, which require additional appraisal steps.
The Avenues (a historic district north of downtown) is popular for walkability and character. Canyon Oaks and Little Chico Creek offer newer construction and quieter streets. South Chico near CSU Chico attracts investors and young buyers. Bidwell Park-adjacent homes carry a premium but the setting is exceptional. Budget matters: older stock in The Avenues can have deferred maintenance, while newer areas trade character for lower upkeep. The Chico neighborhood guide covers each area in detail.
Conditions in Chico in 2026 lean buyer-favorable compared to the peak years. Inventory has increased from the lows of 2021-2022, giving buyers more choices and negotiating room on some properties. Interest rates remain elevated relative to 2020 lows, which has tempered demand and kept prices from running. For buyers who plan to hold for five or more years, Chico's long-term fundamentals (university town, healthcare hub, lower cost than Bay Area) remain solid. Read the July 2026 Chico market update for current data.
Fire insurance availability is the single most important factor to research before making an offer. Many standard carriers do not write policies in the 95969 and 95954 zip codes, so buyers often end up in the California FAIR Plan, which covers the structure but lacks liability and some other standard coverages. Septic systems are common on properties outside town limits, adding inspection and potential replacement costs. Luke has deep experience with both markets. See the Magalia buyer's guide or the Paradise market update for specifics.
In most buyer transactions, Luke's compensation comes from the seller's side of the transaction or is otherwise structured so that buyers pay nothing out of pocket for representation. Since the 2024 NAR settlement changes, buyers sign a Buyer Representation Agreement before touring homes, which outlines exactly how and how much Luke is compensated for that transaction. Luke explains this clearly before you sign anything.

Selling a Home
In mid-2026, average days on market in Chico are running 50 to 80 days from listing to accepted offer, with another 30 to 45 days to close. Well-priced, well-presented homes in the $400,000 to $550,000 range tend to move faster. Homes priced above the local comparable set or with deferred maintenance can sit longer. Total time from listing to funded sale is typically two to four months for a straightforward transaction. Read the Chico seller's guide for the full picture.
Before spending a dollar on repairs, have Luke walk the home first. A clean, decluttered, well-presented home is the baseline every seller should hit before anything else. Beyond that, the right answer depends on your specific property and what buyers in that price range actually care about. Some higher-dollar items add real value to the sale price; others cost thousands and buyers discount them anyway. Luke will walk through with you, tell you what he sees, and help you decide what makes sense for your return on investment versus what you can skip. There is no one-size-fits-all repair list.
Spring (February through April) typically brings the highest buyer activity and the most competitive offer environments in Northern California markets. Summer remains strong, though buyers slow down in July and August partly due to heat. Fall is a solid secondary window. Winter (November through January) has the fewest active buyers but also the fewest competing listings, so motivated buyers and sellers can still transact effectively. In any season, condition and pricing matter more than timing.
A comparative market analysis (CMA) is an analysis of recently sold homes similar to yours, used to estimate your home's current market value. It looks at comparable size, condition, location, and features, adjusted for differences. If you are thinking about selling and want to know what your home would realistically sell for today, Luke can prepare one for you. Reach out here.
Commission in California is negotiated, not fixed by law, and has evolved following the 2024 NAR settlement. Sellers typically pay a listing commission to their own agent. Buyers now sign a Buyer Representation Agreement that outlines agent compensation before touring homes. The actual figures vary by transaction and agent. Luke discusses compensation openly at the beginning of every client relationship so there are no surprises. Read the Orland seller's guide or Chico seller's guide for more context.
Market Conditions
Chico's market in 2026 is roughly balanced, leaning slightly toward buyers compared to 2020-2022. Inventory has risen from historic lows and days on market have extended. Multiple-offer situations still happen on well-priced homes, but buyers generally have time to do due diligence and negotiate on condition items. The market varies by price point: homes under $400,000 see more competition while homes above $700,000 can sit longer and offer more room to negotiate. See the Chico market update for current numbers.
Chico's median home price has been in the range of $475,000 to $505,000 through mid-2026, depending on the month and data source. Prices have moderated from the 2022 peak, though they remain significantly above pre-2020 levels. The most current figures are on the blog market update posts. For a current valuation of a specific property, contact Luke directly.
Higher rates reduce what buyers can afford at any given monthly payment, which reduces competition and puts downward pressure on prices or keeps them flat. In Chico, elevated rates since 2022 have contributed to longer days on market and more seller concessions. They have not caused major price declines because inventory remains relatively low and Chico continues to attract buyers priced out of Sacramento, the Bay Area, and other California metros. Local Areas
Orland is a city in Glenn County and offers some of the most affordable real estate in Luke's service area. Median prices have been in the $280,000 to $330,000 range, attracting first-time buyers and investors. The market is smaller and slower-moving than Chico, with fewer active listings and longer days on market. USDA loans (zero down for eligible buyers) are available in rural Glenn County areas. See the Orland realtor page, the first-time buyer guide for Orland, and the Orland seller's guide for more.
Durham is a small farming community in southern Butte County, about 10 minutes from Chico. It offers a rural feel with agricultural land, lower home prices than Chico, and a tight-knit community. It is popular with buyers who want more land and space while staying close to Chico's amenities. The Durham living guide covers what to expect in detail.

Financing & Costs
It depends on the loan type. FHA loans require 3.5% down with a 580+ credit score. Conventional loans start at 3% for qualifying first-time buyers and 20% to avoid private mortgage insurance. VA loans for eligible veterans require zero down. USDA loans for rural areas like Orland and Glenn County also offer zero-down options. FHA loans are also available in Orland. On a $400,000 home, a 3.5% FHA down payment is $14,000 before closing costs. Down payment assistance is also worth exploring. CalHFA and local programs offer grants and deferred loans that can cover part or all of the down payment for qualifying buyers. Eligibility depends on income, credit, and the property. Luke can connect you with lenders who specialize in these programs. Read the Chico first-time buyer guide for a fuller breakdown.
Minimum scores by loan type: FHA typically requires 580 for maximum financing (3.5% down) or 500-579 for 10% down. Conventional loans generally need 620 or higher, with better rates starting around 740. VA loans have no official minimum but most lenders look for 620+. The higher your score, the better your interest rate and overall loan costs will be.
Buyers in California typically pay 2% to 5% of the purchase price in closing costs beyond the down payment. On a $400,000 home that is roughly $8,000 to $20,000. This covers lender fees, title insurance, escrow fees, homeowner's insurance, and miscellaneous fees such as inspections and appraisal.
FHA loans are available in Paradise and Magalia, but the appraisal process is stricter. FHA appraisers flag health and safety issues more aggressively than conventional appraisals, and any issues must be resolved before the loan closes. Properties with septic systems, well water, or wildfire damage history may require additional inspections. USDA loans are available for many properties in rural Glenn County, including Orland. Talk to Luke about the specific property and he can help you match the right loan type to it.
