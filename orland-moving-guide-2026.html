Moving to Orland, CA: What Buyers Need to Know in 2026

Olive orchards, genuine community, and a lower cost of entry than most Northern California markets. Here's an honest look at Orland's 2026 housing market and what life in this Glenn County town is actually like.

Published October 16, 2026

Orland doesn't appear on many relocation lists, and that tends to work in your favor if you're considering a move here. This Glenn County city of roughly 8,400 residents sits along Interstate 5 in the northern Sacramento Valley, about 20 miles west of Chico and 120 miles north of Sacramento. It's surrounded by the kinds of landscape that define this part of California: olive groves, almond orchards, rangeland, and wide valley views that remind you how much sky exists when you step back from dense development.

Here's what you actually need to know if Orland is on your list.

Where Orland Fits in Northern California

The location does real work for Orland. Sitting along I-5 puts the town inside a useful travel radius: Chico is about 25 minutes east, Redding is roughly 80 miles north, and Sacramento is around two hours south. Red Bluff, Williams, and Willows are all within a short drive. If your work or family ties pull you up and down the I-5 corridor, Orland's position makes a lot of sense.

Black Butte Lake lies about eight miles west of town. Managed by the Army Corps of Engineers, it's a genuine asset for residents: boating, fishing, hiking, and swimming, all within minutes of downtown Orland. The Glenn County Fair comes to town each May, drawing the region together with livestock shows, live music, and the kind of event that doesn't exist in larger markets the same way it does here. For buyers who want more than a house, these are the things that make Orland feel like home.

Glenn County's overall cost of living runs about 17 percent below the California statewide average for a family of three, which is a meaningful difference for buyers calibrating where their income goes farthest. Housing is the largest single driver of that gap.

The 2026 Housing Market in Orland

The median sale price in Orland was about $395,000 in the three months ending August 2026, up 12.8% from a year earlier, as demand from buyers priced out of Chico and the broader Sacramento Valley continues to find its way north and west. That figure is still meaningfully below what comparable properties cost in Chico, and well below what buyers face in coastal California markets.

Homes are taking a median of about 70 days to sell, which is longer than Chico's pace but reflects the more measured tempo of a smaller market rather than lack of demand. Well-priced homes that show well still attract serious attention. The buyers who run into trouble are those who wait too long to get pre-approved or who treat Orland's pace as an invitation to delay decisions on properties they want.

Inventory is limited in the way it is throughout most of Northern California. When a home that checks real boxes comes available, the window to act tends to be shorter than buyers from slower markets may expect. Getting pre-approved before you're actively searching is not optional advice here, it's the difference between being ready and watching someone else close on a house you wanted.

On Interest Rates and Affordability

Rates remain a real factor in every buyer's calculation right now. The practical upside of Orland's price point is that the same rate impact hurts less on a $395,000 purchase than on a $500,000 one. We can sit down, run the actual numbers for your situation, and make sure you're buying at a payment that fits your long-term plan rather than one that just barely works today.

What the Housing Stock Looks Like

Orland's housing inventory reflects the character of a working agricultural town with a modest downtown core. You'll find a mix of older ranch-style homes and mid-century construction in established neighborhoods, some newer builds on the edges of town, and rural properties on larger parcels beyond the city limits. The price range at each tier is meaningful: in-town options on smaller lots tend to start lower, while rural acreage properties come with more land and more variables.

Properties inside the city typically connect to city utilities. Move to the rural fringes of Glenn County and the picture shifts: septic systems are common. If a rural or semi-rural property catches your attention, a full septic inspection during escrow is standard and important. Knowing the condition of those systems before you close protects you from surprises that should have been on the table from the start.

Lot sizes in and around Orland tend to be generous at the price point. Buyers who want room for a garden, animals, outbuildings, or simply space from neighbors will find options in the Orland market that would cost significantly more in Chico or Sacramento-area communities. For buyers with that priority, the trade-off is often an easy call.

First-Time Buyers

Orland's median price gives first-time buyers a lower entry point than Chico, with FHA and USDA loan options often available for properties that qualify. Pre-approval and a clear-eyed sense of priorities matter in any competitive market.

Move-Up Buyers

Buyers relocating from the Bay Area, Sacramento, or even Chico often find Orland offers more home for the budget.

Investors

Orland's rental market is anchored by local workforce demand and proximity to Chico. The lower acquisition cost relative to the Chico market can create favorable conditions, though current rent and vacancy data should drive any investment decision.

What Life in Orland Is Actually Like

Orland functions well at a human scale. It's large enough to have a downtown, local businesses, schools, parks, and civic life, and small enough that you'll recognize faces at the grocery store within a few months of moving in. For buyers coming from larger urban areas who want that shift, Orland delivers it without requiring you to move to a truly remote location.

The Orland Unified School District serves K-12 students, with Orland High School as the town's public high school, and Providence Christian School offers a private option. Families considering Orland often note the smaller class sizes and tight-knit school community as genuine draws, particularly for families coming from larger, more anonymous districts. For college, Butte College has a campus right in Orland as well as its Chico and Oroville campuses, and Chico State is within a reasonable drive.

For shopping and services beyond what Orland's local businesses cover, most residents make regular trips to Chico heading east or to Willows, the Glenn County seat, heading south. Neither drive is onerous. The I-5 corridor makes Redding accessible to the north for specialty shopping, medical care, and other services not available locally. Buyers who work remotely or run their own businesses will find Orland's position particularly flexible.

The agricultural identity of the area is woven into daily life in ways that feel genuine rather than curated. Olive production has been part of Glenn County's economy for well over a century, and you'll find olive oil made locally available in ways that don't exist in most markets. If that kind of regional rootedness is part of what you're looking for in a move, Orland delivers it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Orland CA a good place to live?
For buyers seeking genuine affordability, agricultural character, and a community where people know each other, Orland is worth serious consideration. It's not the right fit for buyers who rely heavily on urban amenities or want to be close to a major metro. For families, remote workers, retirees, and buyers looking for land, Orland checks a meaningful set of boxes that few Northern California markets can match at this price point.
How far is Orland CA from Chico?
About 20 to 25 miles, or roughly 25 to 35 minutes by car on Highway 32 east through the valley. Most Orland residents who work in or regularly visit Chico treat the drive as routine. I-5 and the connecting highways make the route direct and reliable.
What is the median home price in Orland CA?
About $395,000 for the three months ending August 2026 based on Redfin data, up 12.8% from a year earlier. This is noticeably below Chico's market and reflects Orland's position as one of the more accessible markets in Northern California at a meaningful size. Prices vary significantly based on property type, lot size, and whether the home is in-town or on rural acreage.
How long are homes sitting on the market in Orland?
A median of about 70 days for the three months ending August 2026. That's longer than the Chico pace but shorter than some slower rural markets in the region. Well-priced, well-presented homes move meaningfully faster. Buyers should be pre-approved and ready to act when something good comes available, not sorting out financing after the fact.
Are homes in Orland on septic or city sewer?
In-town properties generally connect to city sewer. Rural and semi-rural properties outside the city core are commonly on septic. A thorough septic inspection during escrow is standard for any property on a private system. This is the norm for rural Northern California and something to factor into your due diligence process.
What is the cost of living in Glenn County?
Glenn County's overall cost of living runs about 17 percent below the California statewide average for a family of three, according to Glenn County's own data. Housing is the primary driver of that gap. Utilities, food, and services are comparable to other rural Northern California markets.
