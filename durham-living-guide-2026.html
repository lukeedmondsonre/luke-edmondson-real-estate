Living in Durham, CA: What to Know Before You Move

Small town, big lots, and Chico twelve minutes away. Here's an honest look at what daily life in Durham actually looks like and why it draws buyers who want rural character without real isolation.

Published August 12, 2026

Durham doesn't show up in a lot of relocation headlines. It's not trying to. This small Butte County community of roughly 5,000 residents sits tucked between Chico and Gridley along Highway 99, surrounded by almond orchards, rice fields, and walnut groves. People don't move here by accident. They move here because they've thought carefully about what they want in a home and a community, and what Durham offers has started to make sense.

I'm a Ramsey Trusted Partner and one of Northern California's top-producing Keller Williams agents. I've helped clients buy and sell throughout Butte County for 7+ years, and Durham comes up more and more in conversations with buyers looking for space, community, and a different feel than Chico. Here's what you should actually know before you make a move.

Where Durham Fits in Northern California

Durham sits about 12 miles south of downtown Chico along Highway 99. Gridley is roughly 8 miles to the south. Chico's amenities, including its hospital network, university, retail corridor, and dining options, are close enough to access easily while still living in a quieter, more rural setting. That proximity is a real part of Durham's appeal, and it's one of the main things buyers mention when they're explaining why they landed here.

The geography is flat valley floor: classic Northern California agricultural land with big skies and wide views. If you're coming from a city environment, the quiet can feel significant at first. If that's something you're drawn to rather than worried about, Durham has a lot to offer.

What the Housing Stock Looks Like

Durham's housing market is small. Roughly 30 homes change hands in a typical year, which means inventory is genuinely limited and the market can be hard to read from the outside. When a well-priced home comes up in Durham, it tends to attract real attention from buyers who've been waiting. When something sits, it's often because of pricing, condition issues, or both.

What you'll find here is different from Chico's established neighborhoods. Lots tend to be larger, often a quarter acre to a full acre or more, with room for outbuildings, gardens, or animals. Many properties have older construction, some well cared for and some needing work. You'll find a mix of farmhouses, ranch-style homes, and newer construction on the edges of town. Durham homes can vary widely in price, and it's worth knowing that properties here can run higher than Chico at times, especially when you factor in lot size, acreage, and rural appeal. Working with an agent who knows this market well is important so you understand exactly what you're getting and what it's worth.

A Note on Rural Properties

Many homes in Durham and the surrounding rural areas are on septic systems rather than city sewer. This is standard for the area and not a red flag, but it does mean a septic inspection is an important part of due diligence. Budget for it and don't skip it. A good inspector will tell you exactly what you're working with.

Schools in Durham

Durham Unified School District serves the community and is a consistent draw for families. Durham High School has a solid reputation within Butte County and a small-school environment that many parents find appealing. Class sizes tend to be smaller, teachers know students by name, and the community connection in Durham's schools is something families who've come from larger districts often notice immediately.

Durham Elementary and Durham Middle School round out the K-12 pipeline within the district. For families with kids of varying ages, keeping everyone in the same district and close to home is a practical benefit that comes with Durham's geography. Families considering private or charter options will find more variety in nearby Chico, which is a quick drive from most parts of Durham.

Daily Life: What's Here and What Isn't

Durham has what a small town has: a few local spots, a sense of familiarity, and a pace that reflects the agricultural community it has always been. There's a post office, some local services, and the kind of community where people recognize each other. For the things that require more, Chico is close enough that the gap doesn't feel significant for most families.

Grocery & Essentials

Day-to-day shopping means a short trip to Chico or Gridley. Most Durham residents make this part of their routine without it feeling like a burden. The drive is easy and the roads are straightforward.

Healthcare

Enloe Medical Center in Chico and Oroville Hospital to the east both serve the area. Primary care access in Durham itself is limited, but Chico's healthcare network is among the strongest in the region.

Commuting

Highway 99 makes Chico commutes manageable. For remote workers or those with flexible schedules, Durham works particularly well. Buyers with Sacramento or Bay Area ties should plan for longer drives to those metro areas.

What Durham doesn't have is the density of restaurants, entertainment options, or walkable retail that you'd find in Chico's core. That's not a surprise to buyers who've done their research, and it's often exactly the trade they're looking for. The people who thrive in Durham tend to value the space, the quiet, and the ability to call on Chico when they want more.

Who Is Moving to Durham in 2026

Durham residents can fall into a few categories, and certain types of buyers can be common here. Families drawn to larger lots and a smaller-school environment tend to be a natural fit. Those who appreciate Durham's agricultural character and tight-knit community feel are another. Remote workers who want space and a quieter setting while staying within reach of Chico are also a possibility. These are educated guesses at best. People end up in Durham for all kinds of reasons, and the right fit looks different for everyone.

Buyers with agricultural interests or hobbyist farming in mind also look here. Durham's rural lots can accommodate chickens, small livestock, gardens, and outbuildings in ways that Chico's residential neighborhoods simply don't allow. If that's part of what you're looking for, Durham is one of the places in the region where it's actually possible.

What to Watch For When Buying in Durham

The small inventory in Durham means that when a home you're interested in comes available, moving decisively matters. The flip side is also true: with homes spending around 90 days on market on average in 2026, you have more time to think than you would in Chico's tighter market. The right approach depends on what's actually available when you're ready to move.

Comparable sales in a market this small can be hard to interpret. A handful of sales a year means each transaction has an outsized effect on what "market value" looks like. This is an area where working with an agent who knows rural Butte County well genuinely matters. Applying Chico comps or national data platforms to Durham properties often produces numbers that don't reflect what's actually happening on the ground.

Fire insurance is worth verifying early, particularly for properties with significant acreage or those in areas that have seen elevated fire risk designations in Butte County. It doesn't disqualify homes, but it can affect your monthly carrying costs, and it's better to know before you're under contract than after.

On Pricing and Interest Rates

Durham's price picture is more nuanced than many buyers expect. Homes here can sometimes run higher than comparable Chico properties, particularly when acreage and rural features are factored in. The right move is to look at actual comparable sales, not assumptions. We can sit down, run the real numbers for your situation, and make sure you have a clear picture of what Durham homes are trading for right now.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Durham CA a good place to live?
For the right buyer, yes. Durham is a quiet agricultural community with strong schools, rural character, and close proximity to Chico's amenities. It's not the right fit for everyone, but families, remote workers, and buyers drawn to land and rural lifestyle regularly find it checks the right boxes. It's worth knowing that Durham homes can price higher than Chico at times, so going in with accurate local data matters.
How far is Durham CA from Chico?
About 12 miles, or roughly 15 to 20 minutes depending on where you're headed in Chico. Highway 99 makes the commute straightforward for most routes. Many Durham residents work in Chico and find the drive unremarkable as part of a daily routine.
What are the schools like in Durham CA?
Durham Unified School District serves grades K-12 with a small-school environment that many families value. Durham High School is well regarded within Butte County. Class sizes tend to be small, and the community connection between families and schools is something the district is known for.
Are homes in Durham CA on septic?
Many are, particularly properties with larger lots or those outside the denser core of town. This is common for rural Butte County and is not unusual. A thorough septic inspection during escrow is the right way to evaluate any property on a septic system before you commit.
How is the real estate market in Durham CA right now?
Inventory is limited, with roughly 30 homes sold in a typical year. Days on market have averaged around 90 in 2026. Buyers have more time to evaluate than in faster-paced markets, but well-priced homes still attract attention. The small sample size of comparable sales makes pricing nuance especially important here.
